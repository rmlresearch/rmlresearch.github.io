Course Description

This course introduces generative AI as a new generation of AI systems that create new objects, including text, code, images, audio, video, robot behaviors, 3D shapes, and molecules. Students learn the foundations of AI, machine learning, and deep learning before studying modern generative methods such as transformers, large language models, diffusion models, flow matching, retrieval-augmented generation, and agentic workflows. The course emphasizes both theory and implementation through an interactive course repository, in-person quizzes, one mid-term examination, one cumulative final examination, and a final project in which students build and present a generative AI application.

Course Objectives

  • Explain the core ideas, vocabulary, and mathematical foundations behind modern generative AI systems.
  • Understand and apply major generative model families, including transformers, LLMs, diffusion, and flow matching.
  • Build generative AI for text, code, images, audio, video, robotics, 3D shapes, molecules, agents, and RAG systems.
  • Evaluate generative AI systems for quality, reliability, safety, bias, hallucination, and real-world deployment constraints.
  • Use industry-standard AI coding assistants together with GitHub, Markdown, and project workflows to build, document, and extend course projects.

1Aug. 19I — Foundations What is Generative AI? Historical context, generative vs. discriminative approaches, major modalities and model families. Probabilistic ML basics: random variables, distributions, conditional probability, likelihood, MLE, entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence.
2Aug. 26I — Foundations Formulating the generative AI problem: learning distributions, sampling, conditional generation, density estimation, controllability and evaluation. Latent representations and representation learning: embeddings, latent spaces, autoencoders, VAEs, representation quality.
3Sept. 2II — Text & Code Historical context and first attempts in text modeling: n-grams, statistical language models, RNNs/LSTMs, sequence-to-sequence models and limitations. Recasting text as a generative AI problem: tokenization, autoregressive factorization, next-token prediction, training vs. generation.
4Sept. 9II — Text & Code Transformers and LLMs: attention, Q/K/V, self-attention, transformer blocks, positional information and decoder-only language models. Breakthroughs with scale: pretraining, scaling, zero/few-shot behavior, instruction tuning, preference optimization, prompting and code generation.
5Sept. 16III — Diffusion & Flow Image generation and first attempts: feature inversion/style transfer, autoencoders/VAEs, GANs and autoregressive image models. Diffusion setup: iterative refinement, forward noising, denoising, UNets, conditioning, training and sampling intuition.
6Sept. 23III — Diffusion & Flow Diffusion formalized: forward/reverse processes, noise schedules, denoising objectives, score interpretation and sampling. Flow matching: normalizing-flow intuition, continuous dynamics, probability paths, vector fields and modern flow-matching models.
7Sept. 30IV — Robotics & Simulators Generative AI for robotics: action/trajectory generation, imitation learning, generative policies and diffusion policies. Vision-language-action and embodied foundation models: language-conditioned behavior, multimodal representations and robot control.
8Oct. 7IV — Robotics & Simulators World models and simulators: learned dynamics, generative environments, synthetic experience and model-based reasoning. Generative robotics in practice: simulation, data generation, sim-to-real, safety and evaluation. Project proposal due 11:59 PM.
9Oct. 14V — 3D & Molecules 3D representations and generation: meshes, point clouds, voxels, neural fields, NeRFs and Gaussian splats. Text-to-3D and image-to-3D: reconstruction, generative design, scene/world generation and evaluation.
10Oct. 21V — 3D & Molecules Molecular generation: graphs, SMILES, molecular representations, validity, novelty and property conditioning. Generative science: proteins, structure prediction, diffusion/flow methods for molecules, drug discovery and scientific workflows.
11Oct. 28VI — Agents, RAG & OpenClaw Retrieval-augmented generation: ingestion, chunking, embeddings, vector search, reranking, context construction and citations. Building and evaluating RAG systems: retrieval quality, grounded generation, failure modes, evaluation and practical architectures.
12Nov. 4VI — Agents, RAG & OpenClaw AI agents: tools, planning, reasoning, memory, execution, critics, feedback loops and human-in-the-loop supervision. Agentic systems in practice: coding/research agents, multi-agent workflows, computer use, OpenClaw-style workflows and reproducibility.
13Nov. 11Integration & Recap Synthesis I: Parts I–III — probability, latent variables, autoregression, transformers, diffusion and flow matching as alternative generative formulations. Evaluation, reliability, safety and trustworthy GenAI; project refinement and final-exam preparation.
